The Trip

This is the Peru trip I'd hand someone who wants to see all of it. You'll go from Lima's food scene to the Inca heartland, spend a night steps from Machu Picchu, ride one of South America's most beautiful train routes to Lake Titicaca, and finish with condors over the Colca Canyon and a slower pace in Arequipa. Private guides and drivers handle the details along the way, so all you have to do is take it in.

Krystal Winchel, Founder, Traveling the Atlas

Day by Day

Your itinerary

Day01
Lima

Welcome to Peru

A driver meets you at the airport and takes you to your hotel for two nights. The rest of the day is yours, and Miraflores is the perfect place to ease in with a walk along the clifftop Malecón and your first pisco sour.

Meals: On your ownStay options: Miraflores Park, A Belmond Hotel · JW Marriott Hotel Lima · Country Club Lima Hotel · Pullman Lima Miraflores · Hilton Garden Inn Lima Miraflores
Day02
Lima

Lima with Your Private Guide

Spend the day getting to know the city with your private guide. You'll see Plaza San Martín, the Plaza Mayor and the Government Palace, then step inside Casa de Aliaga, a family home with roots going back to 1535. Next is Museo Larco and its remarkable collection of pre-Columbian art, with lunch at Café del Museo in the museum's gardens. Finish in bohemian Barranco, where street art, galleries and the Puente de los Suspiros set the mood.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay options: Miraflores Park, A Belmond Hotel · JW Marriott Hotel Lima · Country Club Lima Hotel · Pullman Lima Miraflores · Hilton Garden Inn Lima Miraflores
Day03
Lima to the Sacred Valley

Into the Inca Heartland

Your driver takes you to the airport and helps with check-in for the short flight to Cusco, about an hour and twenty-five minutes. From there it's roughly ninety minutes by road down into the Sacred Valley, with a stop at the Sulca textile museum, which recreates the fabrics of ancient Peru, and lunch at the historic Hacienda Huayoccari.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Sacred Valley hotel matched to your style
Day04
Sacred Valley

Maras Salt Pans & Moray

Today your guide takes you deeper into the valley. At the Maras salt pans, local families still harvest salt from hundreds of terraced pools, and the colors shift with the light. Nearby, the circular terraces of Moray are thought to have been an Inca agricultural laboratory, an observatory and a place of worship all at once. Lunch is a traditional meal with the community of Misminay before you return to your hotel.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Sacred Valley hotel matched to your style
Day05
Sacred Valley to Machu Picchu

The Lost City of the Incas

After a short visit to the Inca fortress town of Ollantaytambo, board the train for one of Peru's great rides, as the Andes give way to cloud forest on the way to Aguas Calientes. Your luggage is taken care of while a shuttle bus climbs to the citadel for a guided tour of Machu Picchu. Tonight you sleep right at its gates.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Sanctuary Lodge, A Belmond Hotel, Machu Picchu
Day06
Machu Picchu to Cusco

Sunrise Over Machu Picchu

Set an early alarm, because sunrise over Machu Picchu is the moment people cross the world for. The day is yours to shape: wander the ruins at your own pace, climb Huayna Picchu for the view from above, or soak in the hot springs in Aguas Calientes. In the afternoon, a scenic train returns you to Cusco, where you're met and taken to your hotel.

Meals: BreakfastStay options: Monasterio, A Belmond Hotel · Palacio Nazarenas, A Belmond Hotel · Aranwa Cusco Boutique Hotel · Hilton Garden Inn Cusco
Day07
Cusco

The Archaeological Capital of the Americas

Cusco's cobblestone streets and original Inca walls make it one of the great archaeological cities in the world. Your tour includes Santo Domingo, built on the foundations of the Inca temple of Qorikancha, the San Cristóbal viewpoint, the fortress of Sacsayhuamán, the Inca baths of Tambomachay, and the Plaza de Armas and Cathedral. Freshen up at your hotel, then head back to the plaza for dinner.

Meals: BreakfastStay options: Monasterio, A Belmond Hotel · Palacio Nazarenas, A Belmond Hotel · Aranwa Cusco Boutique Hotel · Hilton Garden Inn Cusco
Day08
Cusco to Puno

The Titicaca Train

An early pickup takes you to Wanchaq Station, where local musicians send you off. The Titicaca Train is one of South America's most scenic rail routes, with big windows and an open-air observation car for the high Andean plains. A three-course lunch with wine and afternoon tea are served in the Pullman-style dining cars, with live music along the way. You're met in Puno and taken to your hotel.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Casa Andina Premium Puno
Day09
Lake Titicaca

Islands of the Highest Lake

Head out by boat to the floating islands of the Uros, built by hand from layers of totora reeds. Continue to Taquile Island, where Quechua-speaking families are known for their extraordinary weaving and close-knit way of life. Lunch is served in a local home, followed by an easy walk through the hills and archaeological sites above the lake. Back in Puno, the evening is yours.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Casa Andina Premium Puno
Day10
Puno to the Colca Canyon

Across the Altiplano

Today is a full travel day of about six hours, and it's a beautiful one. You'll cross the high plateau past the Lagunillas lagoons, home to Andean flamingos and local fishermen, and the Pampa Cañahuas in the Salinas y Aguada Blanca National Reserve, where vicuñas and alpacas graze. Lunch is in the town of Chivay, then it's a short drive to the Colca Lodge for two nights.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Colca Lodge
Day11
Colca Canyon

Condors at Cruz del Cóndor

An early start takes you to the Cruz del Cóndor viewpoint, where, with a little luck, Andean condors ride the morning thermals above one of the deepest canyons on Earth. On the way back, stop in villages ringed by terraces built by the pre-Inca Collagua people, including Maca at the foot of the Sabancaya volcano and Yanque with its baroque colonial church. Spend the afternoon slowing down at the lodge, and don't skip the hot springs.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay: Colca Lodge
Day12
Colca Canyon to Arequipa

On to the White City

After lunch in Chivay, it's about three hours by road to Arequipa, with lookouts, highland villages and the open Pampa Cañahuas along the way. Settle into your hotel and enjoy your first evening in Peru's White City, named for the pale volcanic stone its colonial center is built from.

Meals: Breakfast, LunchStay options: Cirqa · Casa Andina Premium Arequipa · Casa Andina Select Arequipa Plaza
Day13
Arequipa

Arequipa & Santa Catalina

Your private half-day tour starts in the Plaza de Armas, one of the loveliest squares in Peru, and the Cathedral, rebuilt in the 19th century and home to treasures in gold and silver. Visit the Jesuit Church of La Compañía, then step into the Santa Catalina Monastery, a walled city within the city founded in 1579 and closed to the outside world until 1970. Its brightly painted lanes, patios and cloisters are still home to a small community of nuns. The evening is yours.

Meals: BreakfastStay options: Cirqa · Casa Andina Premium Arequipa · Casa Andina Select Arequipa Plaza
Day14
Arequipa to Lima

Homeward

A driver takes you to the airport for your included flight to Lima, where you connect home or extend your trip. Ask me about adding the Amazon or a few beach days on the coast.

Meals: Breakfast

Krystal's Tip

Planning around the altitude

Cusco sits around 11,150 feet and Puno around 12,550, so altitude is the one thing worth planning for. This route starts lower in the Sacred Valley to help you adjust. Keep your first afternoon up high easy, drink plenty of water, and talk with your doctor about altitude medication before you go. Machu Picchu and Huayna Picchu permits are limited and sell out, so booking early gets you the best entry times.
