1. Passports & Visas

A valid passport is required for international travel, and many countries require it to be valid for at least six months beyond your return date and to have blank pages for entry stamps. Please check your passport's expiration date as soon as you start planning, since renewals can take several weeks.

Some destinations require a visa or an electronic travel authorization before you arrive. Entry requirements change often and depend on your citizenship, so please confirm the current rules for every country on your itinerary, including countries where you are only connecting. U.S. citizens can find official requirements at travel.state.gov.

The name on every booking must match your passport exactly. For flights within the United States, you will need a REAL ID compliant license or another accepted form of identification.

2. Travel Protection

We strongly recommend travel protection for every trip. A good plan can help cover trip cancellation or interruption, emergency medical care, medical evacuation, delays, and lost or delayed baggage. Your regular health insurance may offer little or no coverage outside the United States.

Some benefits, such as coverage for pre-existing medical conditions, are only available when a plan is purchased within a set time after your first trip payment. We are happy to walk you through your options, and the decision to purchase or decline coverage is yours.

3. Health & Medical Needs

Some destinations recommend or require specific vaccinations or health precautions. We suggest visiting your doctor or a travel clinic four to six weeks before departure, and reviewing destination guidance from the CDC Travelers' Health site.

If you or anyone in your group has mobility, accessibility, or medical needs, please let us know early. We will look for accommodations and experiences that fit, and share the details suppliers need to prepare for your arrival.

4. Dietary Requirements & Allergies

Tell us about any dietary preferences, restrictions, or food allergies, and we will pass them along to your hotels, restaurants, cruise lines, and tour operators. Suppliers do their best to accommodate requests, but we cannot guarantee how food is prepared.

If you have a serious allergy, carry any medication you may need, and consider bringing an allergy card written in the local language.

5. Traveling with Medication

Pack prescription medications in your carry-on bag, in their original labeled containers, and bring enough for your entire trip plus a few extra days in case of delays. Carrying a copy of your prescriptions, or a note from your doctor, is a good idea.

Some medications that are legal at home are restricted in other countries. If you take a controlled or specialty medication, check the rules for your destination before you go.

6. Flights

Please review your flight details as soon as you receive them and let us know right away if anything looks wrong. Airlines can change schedules after booking, so keep your contact information up to date and check in with your airline before you leave for the airport.

Plan to arrive at the airport at least two hours before domestic flights and three hours before international flights. Baggage allowances and fees vary by airline and fare, so confirm them before you pack.

7. Children Traveling Without Both Parents

When a child travels internationally with only one parent, or with grandparents, relatives, or friends, many countries and airlines may ask for proof that the absent parent or parents have given permission. We recommend carrying a signed consent letter, notarized when possible, along with a copy of the child's birth certificate. Requirements vary by country, so please check before you travel.

8. What to Expect When You Travel

Before you leave, you will receive your itinerary with confirmations, contact details, and the important information for your trip. We recommend keeping digital and printed copies of your passport and travel documents in separate places.

It also helps to learn a little about local customs, tipping, currency, and how you will use your phone abroad. And while you are away, you are never on your own. For arrangements booked through Traveling the Atlas, we are here to help if plans change.

9. Your Privacy

Planning travel means sharing personal details such as passport numbers and health or dietary needs. We use this information only to plan, arrange, and support your travel, share it only with the suppliers who need it, and never sell it. You can read more in our Privacy Policy.
