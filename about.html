My story

It started with a someday of my own.

Sunset on the dunes of Huacachina, Peru. The solo trip I waited years to take, and the reason Traveling the Atlas exists.

Built from a love of travel, not just a business plan.

  1. GROWING UP

    Accidentally first class

    Growing up, I thought vacation meant visiting family. My first flight was to Brownsville, Texas, to see my great-grandparents, who would take us across the border into Mexico. I was so excited to fly for the first time, until my little sister, sitting right next to me, got sick on the plane.

    A flight attendant found her a seat near my dad and asked if I'd mind sitting away from my family. She walked me to the front of the plane and sat me in first class. Snacks, juice, my own seat. I was officially in love with first class.

    A road trip to California for my uncle's wedding showed me mountains, the desert and the Grand Canyon for the first time. When we finally reached the beach, I had a picnic by a rock wall, and the little ground squirrels found my bag of Doritos. Before I knew it, my bag was empty and my heart was full. Those early trips planted something in me. There was so much more out there, and I wanted to see it.

  2. RAISING THREE KIDS

    Small trips, big memories

    Then life happened. I became a single mom of three, and I was raising my youngest brother too. There was never enough money, never enough vacation days, and definitely never enough time.

    Our trips weren't glamorous. We visited family, went to the lake and took weekend road trips. But those little trips gave us something everyday life didn't: uninterrupted time together. Today, some of those ordinary adventures are the memories I treasure most.

    I didn't come from money, and no one handed me a roadmap. I built a business while raising kids, through long days, hard decisions and a work ethic that has always been part of who I am. Eventually, all that work gave me something I hadn't always had: choices.

  3. FINDING ME AGAIN

    Who am I, besides Mom?

    My kids graduated back to back and moved out, and suddenly the house was quiet. For years I knew exactly who I was. I was Mom. When that wasn't my full-time job anymore, I honestly had no idea who I was without them.

    My partner Gary and I started traveling more, and the way I traveled changed. Yes, I still love a first-class seat, an incredible resort, a beautiful spa and a really good cocktail. But I also wanted the little restaurant tourists don't know about. I wanted the culture, the people, the adventure, and a story to bring home.

    Somewhere along the way, I started helping friends plan their trips, finding things they didn't know existed and hearing their stories when they came home. I realized success wasn't just everything I had worked so hard to build. Maybe success was finally having the freedom to enjoy it.

  4. PERU

    My someday, finally

    Then I took a solo trip to Peru. I was nervous about going alone. Peru had been on my list for years, and I finally decided I wasn't going to keep waiting for someone else to go with me.

    So I went. I stood at Machu Picchu. I explored places I'd only seen in photographs. I sandboarded down the dunes and challenged myself. What stayed with me wasn't simply that I'd been to Peru. It was how I felt while I was there. Alive. Curious. Independent. Present. And proud of myself for finally going.

    That's when everything came together. I wanted to help other people stop putting the places they've always wanted to see into the category of "someday."

  5. TODAY

    Traveling the Atlas

    Today I get to travel, experience the world and build a business around something I genuinely love. For someone who started with very little, I don't take that for granted.

    I want to work with people who have spent years building their lives, taking care of everyone else and saying "someday," and help them realize that maybe someday is now. Raising my kids taught me that time goes incredibly fast. You can make more money. You can replace almost anything. But you can't get your time back.

    I'm not just selling travel. I'm helping people make something meaningful out of their time. Because the trip eventually ends. But the memories don't.

You worked hard to build a life worth enjoying. Don't spend the best years of it waiting for someday.WHAT I BELIEVE
