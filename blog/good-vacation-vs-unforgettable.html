The Difference Between a Good Vacation and an Unforgettable One

It isn't how much you spend. It's how intentionally the trip is designed.

BY KRYSTAL WINCHEL  |  JULY 2026  |  4 MIN READ

We've all had a good vacation.

The kind where the weather was nice, the hotel was fine, the food was decent, and you came home rested. You'd do it again. No complaints.

But then there are the other trips. The ones you're still talking about five years later. The ones that show up in your mind randomly on a Tuesday afternoon at work. The ones that changed something in you: the way you see the world, the way you see yourself, or just the bar you now hold every future trip to.

That's the difference between a good vacation and an unforgettable one. And it's not about how much money you spent. It's about intention.

Good Vacations Are Comfortable. Unforgettable Ones Are Intentional.

A good vacation happens when everything goes smoothly. An unforgettable one happens when everything is designed, when someone thought deeply about who you are, what you love, and what kind of experience would genuinely move you.

That looks different for everyone. For one person, it's waking up in a cliffside villa with a private plunge pool and nothing on the agenda but a sunset and a glass of wine. For another, it's summiting something difficult and standing at the top with shaking legs and a view that makes every hard step worth it. For someone else, it's sitting at a tiny restaurant in a town nobody's heard of, eating the best meal of their life.

The common thread isn't the destination. It's that the trip was built for them.

The Details Make the Memory

Think back to the trips that stuck with you. I'd be willing to bet it wasn't the flight or the hotel check-in you remember most. It was a moment. A specific, unexpected, perfectly timed moment you couldn't have scripted.

The local guide who took you somewhere off the map. The restaurant your resort concierge would never have recommended. The sunrise you almost slept through but didn't. The cenote down a dirt road that didn't look real.

Those moments don't happen by accident. They happen because either you, or someone planning specifically for you, took the time to look far beyond the first page of search results. Unforgettable trips are built in the details. The ones most people skip because they ran out of time, ran out of energy, or simply didn't know to look.

It's Not Luxury vs. Budget. It's About Fit.

Here's something I want to be clear about: an unforgettable trip doesn't have to be expensive. And an expensive trip isn't automatically unforgettable.

I've had breathtaking moments at gorgeous all-inclusive resorts where everything was handled and perfect and I didn't want to leave. I've also had breathtaking moments in tiny guesthouses in small towns where the night cost almost nothing.

What made both unforgettable was that they were right. Right for that moment, right for what I needed, right for where I was in life and what I was looking for. Memories aren't defined by thread count or price tags. They're defined by that deep, undeniable feeling that the entire experience was crafted uniquely for you.

Most People Plan Logistics. Few People Plan the Experience.

This is where most vacations stay in good territory and never quite make it to unforgettable. The flights get booked. The hotel gets picked. Maybe a few restaurants get researched. Then you show up and figure out the rest, which usually means doing whatever is most convenient, most visible, or most recommended by the front desk.

There's nothing wrong with that. But it leaves a lot on the table. Unforgettable trips happen when someone asks different questions from the start. Not just where do you want to go, but what experience do you want? What kind of traveler are you? What have you always wanted to do but never quite made happen? What would make you come home and immediately start telling everyone you know?

Those answers shape a completely different kind of trip. That's the real benefit of working with a travel advisor: someone whose entire job is planning the experience, not just the logistics.

You Deserve the Unforgettable Kind

Life is short and the world is enormous. There are experiences out there, places, moments, and adventures, that will genuinely change you. And most of them are more accessible than you think. They just require someone who knows where to look and how to put it all together.

At Traveling the Atlas, that's exactly what I do. Whether you're picturing a luxury travel experience where every detail is handled, a hidden gem adventure most people never find, or something in between, I want to help you build the kind of trip you'll still be talking about years from now.

Good vacations are great. But unforgettable ones? Those are what travel is really for.
